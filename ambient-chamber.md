AMBIENT CHAMBER

Why the chamber's signals reach a visitor without being decoded.

---

What this is

The chamber communicates through bodies, colour, breath, and movement — not through text or numbers. This file records the claims that design rests on, and is honest about which claims are supported and which are not.

This is a reference document. It does not describe the chamber's behaviour. The design notes do that.

---

The signals

Five channels, each carrying one layer. Chosen for strength of evidence, not for variety.

Channel Carries Layer
Pupil size Attention 9
Movement smoothness Strain 12
Breath depth Mood 11
Rim warmth Valence 10
Water tint Valence (ambient) 24

Every channel is already drawn in the code. The change is that they are currently either static or driven by the wrong variable. Wiring them to real state is the work.

---

The claims

1. Valence is readable from movement.
People reliably read positivity and negativity from biological motion, even from point-light displays with no body outline. The features that carry it — velocity, acceleration, vertical movement, contraction — are the ones the chamber modulates.

Evidence: well-established.

2. Pupil dilation signals attention and arousal.
Pupils dilate involuntarily with cognitive load, interest, and affective response. Cannot be faked. Detected pre-consciously by observers. Universal across mammals with eyes.

Evidence: strongest in this file.

3. Colour carries affect across cultures.
Warm colours associate with activation and approach; cool colours with withdrawal and calm. The association is broadly universal, not a Western convention.

Evidence: moderate. See caveat 1.

4. Ambient signals are absorbed without being understood.
An ambient display study found the display changed participants' behaviour, and participants were unaware of the change. Nonverbal channels work better when not consciously decoded.

Evidence: strong. This is the claim the whole design rests on.

5. Valence–arousal is the right model.
The two-axis model is the dominant framework in affective computing. Building on a single valence axis is standard practice.

Evidence: well-established.

6. Body form changes how emotion reads.
Emotions conveyed by non-human forms are perceived differently from human ones at a visceral level. The creatures' anatomies — turtle, jelly, crab, angel, seahorse — are doing perceptual work, not decoration.

Evidence: supported, with the caveat that it constrains how the signals should be built, not whether they work.

---

The caveats

1. Colour has cultural specificity.
Universal patterns exist, but there is also real culture-specificity. A visitor whose culture reads white as mourning will read the cool end of the tint differently. The tint stays subtle because of this.

2. Ambient signals can be misread.
The same study notes ambient displays may be misinterpreted due to cultural and physiological differences. Signals must be unambiguous in direction. All five creatures move together or the visitor reads wrongness without being able to name it.

3. The research supports the mechanism, not the strength.
No study says how many degrees of rim warmth is enough. That is tuning, and it will take passes.

---

What this means in practice

The target is not that the visitor understands. The target is that the visitor feels something without knowing why.

A visitor who stays one minute may sense the chamber is calm or uneasy. A visitor who returns tomorrow will feel a difference. Feeling requires comparison. Comparison requires memory. The affect layer is slow by design — it does not work in a screenshot, it works over a session.

Two audiences, two channels:

· Pulse and body signals — visible to the attentive visitor, invisible to the casual one.
· Water tint and atmosphere — felt by everyone, decoded by no one.

Neither audience sees the wrong thing. The attentive visitor sees machinery. The casual visitor sees weather.

---

Not yet grounded

The following are design intentions, not claims backed by research:

· That pattern or chromatophore change can carry meaning to a human observer
· That agreement and disagreement between the council can be read from their arrangement
· That the 33 layers can be revealed through speech without being announced

These are worth testing. They are not evidence.

---

Consistency is the constraint

The failure mode is signals that do not match. A warm rim on a surprised creature. A still pattern on a busy one. When channels disagree, the visitor reads wrongness without being able to name it — and the chamber stops feeling like a place.

Same signal. Same meaning. Across all five creatures, every time.

---

What this file does not do

It does not describe the chamber's behaviour. It does not list the code. It does not settle the tuning.

It records the claims the design stands on, so a future thread knows which parts were chosen because the science supports them, and which were chosen because they seemed right.

Both are honest. Only one is evidence.
